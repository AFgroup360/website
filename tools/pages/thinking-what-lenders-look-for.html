Financing

What a lender is actually reading when they say no.

Strong businesses get declined on presentation more often than on performance. The file has to survive somebody who has never met you.

A credit decision is made by people who will never walk your floor. They have your numbers, whatever you wrote to explain them, and an obligation to assume the worst where something is unclear. Ambiguity does not read as neutral. It reads as risk.

The four questions behind every file

Whatever the format, a lender is working through the same short list.

  • Can it service the debt. Not can it be profitable in a good year, but what does the cash actually do, month by month, through a bad one.
  • What is already ahead of me. Every loan, lease, tax arrear and supplier balance, and who gets paid first if things tighten.
  • What is the money for, and is that the right amount. A number that was hoped for rather than measured is visible immediately.
  • Do these numbers hold up. Whether the file reconciles to source, and whether the person presenting it knew the answer before being asked.

Where good businesses lose

Most declines we see are not about the underlying business at all.

Books built for a tax return, which answer a different question. Owner and related party movement mixed into operating cash, so nobody can tell what the business itself produces. A liability that surfaces late, which puts every other figure in doubt. A request sized to what the owner wanted rather than to the gap that was measured.

Each one is fixable before anybody outside sees it. None of them is fixable in the week the answer is due.

By the time a lender is asking the question, the answer is whatever your records already say. The work has to happen before that.

What being ready looks like

It looks like a document that puts the verdict on the first page and then proves it. Two years of cash month by month with operating separated from everything else. Every obligation listed with terms and priority. A use of funds sized against a measured gap. The conditions a lender would attach, stated before they attach them.

That is the Financing Readiness Review, and the reason to have one is not that you are borrowing. It is that the same file tells you where your own business actually stands.
