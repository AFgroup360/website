Cash

Why a profitable month can still leave you short.

Profit is an opinion about timing. Cash is a fact about today. Most owners get caught in the space between them.

Your accountant shows you a month that made money. Your bank balance says otherwise. Both are correct, and the reason is that they are answering different questions.

Profit asks whether the work you did was worth more than what it cost. Cash asks whether the money has actually arrived. On an income statement a sale counts the day you invoice it. In the bank it counts the day it clears. In between sits every problem we get called about.

The four places they come apart

When a profitable business runs out of money, it is almost always one of these, and usually more than one at once.

  • Receivables. You have earned it and recorded it, but the customer has not paid. The profit is real. The cash is a promise.
  • Inventory. Money converted into things on a shelf. It does not touch the income statement until it sells, so a big buy makes no dent in profit and a large one in cash.
  • Debt principal. Interest is an expense. Principal is not. A business can repay a great deal of money every month and see none of it on the profit line.
  • Owner draws and related party movement. Cash leaves. Profit does not move.

Add capital spending and prepaid costs and you have the full set. None of it is exotic. All of it is invisible if the only number you look at each month is the bottom of the income statement.

A business does not fail because it stopped being profitable. It fails because it ran out of cash while it was still profitable.

What to look at instead

The useful question is not what did we make. It is what is going to be in the account, and when. That takes three things.

  • A forward cash view that runs weeks ahead, not a bank balance that reports this morning.
  • A reconciliation from profit to cash each month, so the gap is explained rather than noticed.
  • Receivables and payables watched by age, because the timing between them is the whole game.

None of that replaces your bookkeeper or your accountant. It is the layer that sits after the recording and before the decision, and in most owner led businesses nobody owns it.
