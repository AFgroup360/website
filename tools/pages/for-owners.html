Most of it is a timing problem.

Owners often assume tight cash means a weak business. Usually it means money leaves before it arrives.

2

What you notice, and what it usually is.

Owners describe symptoms. The cause is almost always one of a short list.

What you notice What it usually is What we do about it
Cash feels tight in a good month Money leaving before it arrives, not a profit problem Map the cycle and forecast it forward, week by week

3

What this looks like in practice.

A recent engagement, with the details removed.

Illustrative. Client details withheld.
The business
Owner led. Revenue up roughly 50% in a year and profitable. Nothing wrong with the business itself.
The setup
Payroll run by hand. No integrated accounting system. Nobody whose job was the numbers.
The squeeze
Large supplier costs paid up front, customer payments arriving 30 to 45 days later. The owner was covering that window personally and reaching for an overdraft.
What we found
A balance sheet account sitting at a large negative balance that cannot be negative. Years of uncategorised transactions in one place. Any lender who opens that file closes it.
The leak
Roughly five figures a month going out for capacity the business was not using. The owner knew. Nobody had ever put a number on it.
What changed
Books rebuilt from source records. The leak sized and cut. Cash mapped forward on a rolling basis. The financing request rebuilt into something a credit committee could approve, at the amount the business actually needed rather than the amount first asked for, and taken to more than one lender.

A twelve month engagement in three phases. It starts with the financing and the cash, then fixes the plumbing, then keeps running.

Days 1 to 45 Days 46 to 120 Ongoing

Get the money right

  • Rebuild the history and clear whatever is sitting in the wrong place

Phase two

Fix the plumbing

  • Put in a real accounting system, connected to the bank

  • Build a month end close that actually closes
  • Discipline on who gets paid when, and on collections

Phase three

Run it with you

  • Budget against actual, so you see drift early
