About

What we've actually done.

AmeriFinancial is a Toronto finance firm that sits between your bookkeeper and your accountant: cash you can see coming, reporting you can run on, and numbers a bank can work with.

Somebody has to look at the cash before it's gone, get a straight number in front of the owner every month, and keep the bank calm. The hard part is doing that when the business is under pressure. We've done it in three very different places. Here's the shape of the work, with the names left out.

A food producer, Tunisia

It was in trouble. We steadied it.

What was going on
A food producer with its own farm, in real trouble. Cash went out faster than anyone tracked it. The lenders wanted answers. And nobody inside could pull a reliable number, so every conversation started with a guess.
What we did
We came in as chief restructuring officer, which means we ran the finance side, day to day. Cash first: what's in the bank, what leaves this week, who gets paid. The lenders got straight numbers and one person to call. Reporting started landing on time.
What changed
It held. Cash was under control, the lenders knew where things stood, and management could open a report and believe it. That's the floor. Everything else gets built on it.
A family business, Uganda

Suppliers got paid when customers paid.

What was going on
A family business with no finance department. Nobody's job was to watch the money. It paid suppliers before its customers paid it, borrowed to cover the stretch, and paid interest on every day of it. Accountants call that stretch the cash conversion gap.
What we did
We built the finance department from nothing: the routines, the reviews, someone whose job it was to know. Then we sat down with the suppliers and negotiated terms, so the day a supplier got paid lined up with the day a customer paid.
What changed
The gap closed. Money came in when it needed to go out, the business borrowed less, and the interest bill came down. It was never a weak business. Just a timing gap.
Owner-led businesses, Toronto

Grew faster than the books did.

What was going on
Two patterns from our files. Nobody did anything wrong. A business growing faster than its books: money leaking on things it never used, and a financing request sized wrong for any lender. And a services business whose owner couldn't see next month's cash.
What we did
Same job, closer to home. Found the leaks and priced them. Rebuilt the financing ask at a size the business could carry. Built a cash forecast the owner actually looks at, and a monthly report they can run the business on.
What changed
One owner now has a financing request a lender can take seriously, and knows what the leaks were costing. The other can see a tight month before it lands. Both run on numbers they trust.

Three places, three problems, one job underneath: get the cash under control, get the numbers right, keep the business moving.

The approach

Bookkeeping looks back. We look forward.

A bookkeeper records what already happened, and we don't replace that. We work on what comes after it. Once a month we go through the cash position, the margins and the management report with you, working from read access to the books you already keep. QuickBooks, Sage, Xero, spreadsheets. Nothing gets migrated and nothing gets disrupted.

Your business is the boat. We're the floaties strapped to the side, keeping it from wobbling until it gets where it's going.
