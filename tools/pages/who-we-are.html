The work

What that looks like in practice.

A business preparing for financing

The owner needs capital. The numbers on hand are built to file a tax return, not to support a credit decision.

The situation
  • No reliable statement of the real financial position
  • Obligations and arrears not fully mapped
  • Nobody can say what the business could realistically service
What we do
  • Establish the position from source documents
  • Complete a Financing Readiness Review
  • Identify what is affecting financeability
  • Shape a structure the business can carry
What changes
  • The financing conversation runs on numbers both sides trust
  • Conditions are known and addressed before they are imposed
  • The reporting and control stay in place afterwards

A growing business whose finance function has not kept pace

Revenue is climbing. The reporting, systems and controls are still the ones built when the business was half the size.

The situation
  • Month end arrives too late to be useful
  • No forward view of cash
  • Working capital absorbs more with every new job
  • Bigger decisions made on thinner information
What we do
  • Build cash forecasting the business can rely on
  • Put in a monthly close that holds
  • Work receivables, payables and terms properly
  • Set controls to match the size the business has reached
What changes
  • Management has a current view of the business
  • Growth is funded deliberately rather than absorbed
  • A finance function able to carry the next stage

Some of this work starts with the owner. Some of it starts with the capital provider. What gets built is much the same either way.
