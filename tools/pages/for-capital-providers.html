For lenders

Know what you are funding, and what happens to it after.

You send us a file you're looking at. We go into the business, build the numbers back up from the bank statements, and tell you what's really there. What they can carry, what they can't, and what needs fixing before the money goes out.

What we check before the money goes out.

What we collect

The raw records, not summaries.

  • Bank, credit card and operating line statements
  • Accounting system and bookkeeping data
  • Filed tax returns and where they stand with CRA
  • Balance sheet, income statement, debt stack and amounts outstanding
How we analyse it

We start at the bank statements.

Bookkeeping is built to file a tax return. Different job. We're working out what this business can actually carry, and how the deal needs to be shaped around that.

  • Two years of cash flow, month by month, so you can see where the money went and where it's leaking
  • How deep the hole is, and whether they can climb out of it
What you get back

Something you can actually lend against.

  • Whether this is financeable at all
  • The number that actually fixes it, which often isn't the number they asked for
  • What conditions belong in the deal before it funds
  • Terms they can realistically pay back
  • A view on what the business is worth

Get those three right and you're not going on gut. You know the number's right, you know what to fix first, and you know they can carry it.

What we watch every month after the money's out.

The risk doesn't end at close. We stay on the file for the life of the loan, so problems surface while there's still something you can do about them.

Keeping an eye on it

Cash and what's due.

  • Cash forecast, updated on a set schedule
  • What's coming due, before it comes due
  • Who owes them, who they owe, month over month
  • Whether they're holding to the conditions in the agreement
Making the money work

Putting it to use.

Watching a loan doesn't protect it. Most of what we do after funding is making sure the money you put in gets used the way the deal assumed it would.

  • Working capital managed month to month, since that's usually what the money is carrying
  • Spending kept to the plan the loan was built on
  • The business pushed toward being worth more by the end of the term than it was at the start

It's how development banks lend. The money goes out, but people stay in, and somebody actually helps the business perform.

Most firms build the model and disappear.

A model is a photo of one day. Whatever made the deal look good at close is the same thing worth watching for the next three years. We stay on it the whole way, so nobody's making calls on numbers from eighteen months ago.
