For lenders

Know what you are funding, and what happens to it after.

Lenders bring us a file they are considering. We go into the business, build the current financial position from source records, and report on the financial health of the company: what it can carry, what it cannot, and what has to change before you release funds.

1

What we check before you fund.

We work alongside management and whoever keeps their books to pull the underlying records, then build the picture from the source data rather than from what has been reported.

What we collect

Source records, not summaries.

  • Bank, credit card and operating line statements
  • Accounting system and bookkeeping data
  • Filed tax returns and CRA account position
  • Balance sheet, income statement, debt stack and amounts outstanding
How we analyse it

Bank statement led, not bookkeeping led.

Bookkeeping exists for tax filing and reporting. We are working out what the business can actually carry, and how the financing needs to be shaped around it.

  • 24 months of trending cash flow, so you can see where the money went and where it is leaking
  • Balance sheet analysis showing how deep the liquidity gap runs, and whether it is recoverable
What you get back

A position you can lend against.

  • Whether the business is financeable at all
  • The amount that actually closes the gap, which is not always the amount requested
  • Conditions that belong in the deal before it funds
  • Structure and terms the business can realistically repay
  • A valuation view to support the decision

Get those three right and the file stops being a judgement call. You know the number is the right number, you know what has to be fixed before it funds, and you know the business can carry what you are about to hand it.

2

What we watch every month after you fund.

The risk does not end at close. Financial oversight continues for the life of the facility, so the position stays visible while there is still room to act on it.

Keeping the position visible

Cash and obligations.

  • Rolling cash forecast, refreshed on a fixed cycle
  • Obligations coming due, before they come due
  • Receivables and payables ageing, month over month
  • Conditions of the deal tracked against the agreement
Making the money work

Working capital and value.

Watching a loan does not protect it. Most of what we do after funding is making sure the money you advanced is put to work the way the deal assumed.

  • The working capital cycle managed month to month, since that is usually what the funding is carrying
  • Cash flow control, so spending follows the plan the financing was built on
  • Performance steered toward a stronger business and a better valuation over the term

It is the model development banks use when they lend. The money goes out, but people stay in, and the business is actively helped to perform.

3

Most firms hand over a model and leave.

Plenty of firms will build the diagnostic. Very few stay on the file once the money moves.

A model is a snapshot of one moment. The things that made a deal sound at close are the same things that need watching for the next three years. We carry the work all the way through, so the file is never being judged on numbers that have gone stale.
