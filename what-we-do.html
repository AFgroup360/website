We put the reporting and controls in place, then help you use them.

So management can see where the business stands, what is coming next, and decide with better information than they had before.

For business owners

How the work is scoped.

Three levels of involvement. Which one fits depends on how much finance capability is already inside the business.

Financial visibility

You have a team running day to day. What is missing is a clear, current picture.

  • Monthly reporting you can act on
  • Cash visibility and forecasting
  • A recurring review of the numbers
  • A straight answer on where the business stands

Financial control

We are in the numbers with you through the month, not just reporting on it afterwards.

  • Everything in financial visibility
  • Cash and payments managed in month
  • Working capital actively worked
  • Controls and process put in place
  • Support on the decisions as they come up

Financing and growth readiness

Something significant is coming. Financing, investment, expansion or a sale.

  • Everything in financial control
  • The Financing Readiness Review
  • Preparation for lender and investor conversations
  • Modelling for major financial decisions

Scope and fees are confirmed after an introductory call, once we know what the business actually needs.

Capital goes in on what somebody can see. It comes back on how the business is run.

For capital providers

Before the money goes in, and after.

The same financial control work, applied first to understanding the business and then to keeping it visible.

Before funding

Understand the business.

  • The real financial position, built from source records
  • Financial risks and issues surfaced early
  • Financeability and what the business can service
  • Debt and obligations, with terms and priority
  • What the financing is actually for
  • Conditions that may need addressing first

The transaction Capital is deployed

After funding

Keep it controlled and visible.

  • Recurring management reporting
  • Cash flow monitoring
  • Working capital oversight
  • Financial controls maintained
  • Ongoing visibility into how the business is performing
  • Regular updates to the capital provider

This is not oversight of the borrower. A business with better financial control is a stronger business, which is what both sides wanted from the outset.

The deliverable

The Financing Readiness Review.

One document that states where the business stands and what would have to be true for capital to go in. Written so the owner and the lender can read the same page.

What is inside it.

The verdict

Financeable, not financeable, or financeable on conditions. Stated on the first page with the conditions listed.

Historical cash position

Operating cash flow over the period reviewed, with owner and related party movement separated out.

Debt and obligations

Loans, leases, tax arrears and supplier balances, with amounts, terms and where each one sits in the queue.

Use of funds

What the financing is actually for, sized against the gap we measured rather than the number that was hoped for.

Conditions

What a lender would reasonably attach, set out before they attach it.

What we verified

Figures tied back to source documents, with the method stated.

What remains open

Anything that could not be tied out is disclosed rather than smoothed over.
