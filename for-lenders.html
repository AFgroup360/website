For lenders

Know what you are funding — and what happens to it after.

Lenders bring us a file they are considering. We go into the business, build the financial position from source records, and tell you what is actually there. If the facility goes ahead, we stay on it for the life of the loan.

01

What we check before you fund.

We work alongside management and whoever keeps their books to pull the underlying records, then build the picture from the source data rather than from what has been reported.

What we collect

Source records, not summaries.

  • Bank, credit card and operating line statements
  • Accounting system and bookkeeping data
  • Filed tax returns and CRA account position
  • Balance sheet, income statement, debt structure
How we analyse it

Bank-statement-led, not bookkeeping-led.

Bookkeeping exists for tax filing and reporting. We are gauging whether the business can carry the debt.

  • 24 months of trending cash flow — where the money went, and where it is leaking
  • Balance sheet analysis — how deep the liquidity gap runs and whether it is recoverable
  • The business modelled against its own industry and operating reality
What you get back

A position you can lend against.

  • Whether the business is financeable at all
  • The amount that actually closes the gap — which is not always the amount requested
  • Conditions that belong in the deal before it funds
  • Structure and terms the business can realistically repay
  • A valuation view to support the decision

A request sized at the wrong number, or funded without the right conditions attached, is a problem that surfaces later — on your side of the table.

02

What we watch every month after you fund.

The risk does not end at close. Financial oversight continues for the life of the facility, so the position stays visible while there is still room to act on it.

Cash and obligations

  • Rolling cash forecast, refreshed on a fixed cycle
  • Obligations coming due, before they come due
  • Receivables and payables ageing, month over month

Performance against the file

  • Actual results against the forecast that supported the request
  • Conditions of the deal tracked against the agreement
  • Reporting in a form built for review, not reconstruction

Deterioration shows up in the reporting early, while options still exist.

03

Most firms hand over a model and leave.

Plenty of firms will build the diagnostic. Very few stay on the file once the money moves.

A model is a snapshot of one moment. The conditions that made a deal sound at close are the conditions that need watching for the next thirty-six months. We carry the same work through the whole life cycle of the financing — the diagnostic before it funds, and the oversight after — so the file is never being assessed on numbers that have gone stale.
